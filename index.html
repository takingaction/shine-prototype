What a partnership looks like

We bring the curriculum. You bring the room.

SHINE is a true partnership. Here's exactly who does what.

We bring

  • The full SHINE curriculum and lesson framework
  • Brian Feinstein as lead artist and music director
  • Sheet music, lyric frameworks, and production playbook
  • Marketing assets (photography, video, copy, host toolkit)
  • Liability insurance and COI documentation
  • Post-show debrief and a recorded copy of the performance

You bring

  • Rehearsal space large enough for 20 students to move
  • Performance venue for the Friday/Saturday opening
  • A piano or full-size keyboard
  • On-site teaching assistant(s), typically 1-2 adults
  • Promotion, registration, and ticketing through your channels
  • Families, donors, and press (your audience)
A typical host week

The 6-day journey from a blank page to opening night.

Brian leads each day. Your team supports on the ground.

Day 1 · The Blank Page

Ensemble forms, ideas fly.

Brian: leads creative games and story prompts. Host team: greets families, runs morning logistics.

Day 2 · Characters and Story

The group votes on the story.

Brian: facilitates the writing of plot and characters. Host team: teaching assistants support small-group work.

Day 3 · Writing the Songs

Original music is born.

Brian: composes at the piano with students. Host team: documents the process for marketing and families.

Day 4 · Scenes and Staging

Dialogue meets blocking.

Brian: writes dialogue and stages scenes. Host team: arranges the venue for tech rehearsals.

Day 5 · Rehearsal

Polish. Costumes. Props.

Brian: runs full run-throughs. Host team: coordinates costumes, programs, and front-of-house.

Day 6 · Opening Night

Your venue. Their bows.

Brian: directs the performance and supports the cast. Host team: runs box office, houses the audience, hosts the reception.

FAQ

The questions hosts - and parents - ask most.

How far in advance do we need to book?

Most hosts book 3–6 months in advance. Summer intensives typically confirm by February; school-year intensives by the start of the prior term.

What kind of space do we need?

A rehearsal room large enough for 20 students to move, and a theater or auditorium for the final performance with lighting, a screen for projections, and seating for an audience of family and friends.

Does Brian travel?

Yes. Brian leads intensives on-site at host venues. Travel is included in the partnership quote.

Do we need a piano?

An acoustic piano or full-size keyboard is required. Brian composes and rehearses at the instrument throughout the week.

How is the program marketed?

FeinLine provides marketing assets (photography, video clips, copy, and a host toolkit). Your team handles registration, ticketing, and local outreach to your community.

What about insurance and liability?

FeinLine carries professional liability insurance and can provide a COI naming your organization as additional insured for the intensive week.

What is the financial model?

Partnership pricing varies by cohort size, format, and travel. Most hosts use a combination of registration tuition and sponsorship. Inquire for a tailored quote.

Does my child need singing, acting, or songwriting experience?

No. SHINE is designed for first-timers and seasoned performers alike. All students learn songwriting from scratch with a professional composer at the piano.

What ages is SHINE for?

SHINE is open to ages [X–Y]. The cohort is one ensemble of about 20 students working together, regardless of experience level.

What does a typical day look like?

Days run [9:00 AM – 3:00 PM]. Mornings are writing and rehearsal; afternoons are scene work and staging. Lunch and snacks are arranged by the host.

Will the performance be recorded?

Yes. Families receive a recording of the final performance along with the original script the ensemble created.

What should my child bring?

A water bottle, a packed snack if you have dietary restrictions, and clothes they can move in. Script, materials, and SHINE T-shirt are all provided.

What happens to the musical they create? Who owns it?

The original script and songs remain part of the ensemble's portfolio. Each student receives a printed copy and a recording as a keepsake.
